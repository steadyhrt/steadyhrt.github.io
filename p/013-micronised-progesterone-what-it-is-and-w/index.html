Progesterone: HRT's other hormone

If you have a uterus and take estrogen for menopause, you'll often need a second hormone alongside it. Here's what that hormone is and why.

01 · The pairing

Why estrogen doesn't come alone

  • If you still have a uterus, estrogen is combined with a progestogen
  • Taking both helps protect against the risk of womb cancer
  • This combination is called menopausal hormone therapy (MHT)
02 · What it is

A progestin — a female hormone

  • Progesterone belongs to a class of medications called progestins
  • In HRT, it works by reducing estrogen's effect on the uterus
  • In the US, the oral capsule form is available under the brand name Prometrium
03 · How it's taken

A capsule, often at bedtime

Oral progesterone comes as a capsule. It's often prescribed for the evening or at bedtime — a timing choice linked to its potential to cause drowsiness in some people.

04 · Side effects

What to be aware of

  • Dizziness and drowsiness are possible — worth flagging to your doctor
  • Headache, breast tenderness, tiredness, and mood swings are also listed
  • Severe or unexpected symptoms should be reported to your doctor promptly
Bring this to your appointment

Questions for your doctor

  • Given my history, do I need a progestogen alongside my estrogen?
  • Is an oral progesterone capsule the right option for me, or are there others?
  • How should I handle side effects like drowsiness or mood changes?
