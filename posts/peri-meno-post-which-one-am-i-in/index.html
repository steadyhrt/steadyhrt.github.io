IF THE TERMS CONFUSE YOU

Peri, meno, post: which one am I in?

Perimenopause is the run-up, menopause comes next, then postmenopause. Here is how the NHS sets them out.

THE THREE TERMS

How the NHS defines each one

  • Perimenopause ends when you reach menopause
  • Menopause is when 12 months go by with no period
  • After this it's called postmenopause
HOW PERIMENOPAUSE OFTEN SHOWS UP

A change to your periods is often an early sign

The NHS says a change to your periods is usually one of the first signs. They can come more or less often, and bleeding can get heavier or lighter.

IF YOU ARE PAST MENOPAUSE

Symptoms can continue after menopause

The NHS says some symptoms, such as joint pain and vaginal dryness, can continue after menopause, into postmenopause.

IF YOU USE HORMONAL CONTRACEPTION

It can blur the picture

The NHS says hormonal contraception can make it harder to tell whether you have reached perimenopause or menopause, because it can affect your periods.
