IF YOUR HRT HAS TWO HORMONES

Why is a progestogen in my HRT too?

If you still have your womb, the NHS says taking both hormones helps protect against womb cancer.

THE MAIN REASON

With a uterus, estrogen comes with a partner

For HRT tablets, patches, gel or spray, the NHS says a uterus means both hormones, not estrogen alone. The NHS says low-dose vaginal estrogen can be used without a progestogen, even with a womb.

IF YOU'VE HAD A HYSTERECTOMY

Not everyone needs the second hormone

  • The NHS recommends estrogen-only HRT once the uterus has been removed
  • If you have not had a hysterectomy, the NHS says estrogen gel must be paired with a progestogen
HOW THE SECOND HORMONE ARRIVES

More than one way to get it

  • One option is progesterone, a capsule you take by mouth (MedlinePlus)
  • The NHS says a progestogen can also come from tablets, or from a Mirena coil, which releases levonorgestrel
  • Some HRT products already contain both hormones in one
WHY YOUR SCHEDULE LOOKS LIKE THAT

Cyclical or every day

  • The NHS says cyclical (sequential) HRT is usually recommended if you still have periods
  • The NHS says continuous HRT, with both hormones daily, is usually recommended once you haven't had a period for a year
  • On a cyclical plan, the NHS says a period at the end of each cycle is expected
Bring this to your appointment

Questions for your doctor

  • Do I need a progestogen with my estrogen, given my history?
  • Is a cyclical or continuous routine right for my stage?
  • What should I expect for bleeding on this plan?
